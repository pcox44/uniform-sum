Uniform Sum Simulation

Process: Generate independent random numbers from a uniform distribution on [0,1]. Lay the “sticks” end-to-end until the running total exceeds the target. The simulation tracks how many sticks are needed and compares the average to the theoretical value when available.

We generate independent uniform random variables \(X_1, X_2, X_3, \dots \sim \mathrm{Uniform}(0,1)\), and let \[ S_n = X_1 + X_2 + \cdots + X_n \] be their running total. Define the stopping time \[ N = \min \{ n : S_n > 1 \}, \] the first stick that pushes the total past 1.

The probability that exactly \(n\) sticks are needed is \[ \mathbb{P}(N = n) = \frac{1}{n!}. \] Therefore, the expected number of sticks is \[ \mathbb{E}[N] = \sum_{n=1}^{\infty} n \cdot \frac{1}{n!} = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!} = e. \] This explains why simulations cluster around \(e \approx 2.718\).

Geometric / Simplex Explanation

The condition \(S_n \le 1\) describes an \(n\)-dimensional simplex: \[ \{(x_1, \dots, x_n) : x_i \ge 0,\; x_1 + \cdots + x_n \le 1\}. \] Geometrically, this simplex contains all sequences of \(n\) sticks whose sum stays under 1. Its volume is \(\mathrm{Vol} = \frac{1}{n!}\), which equals the probability that the first \(n\) sticks do not exceed 1.

You can think of the \((n+1)\)-th stick as the “height” above the simplex — the fraction of this height beyond 1 is exactly the chance it pushes the total past the target. In this way, the simplex volume gives \(\mathbb{P}(N = n+1) = 1/n!\), connecting the geometry directly to the stopping probability.
